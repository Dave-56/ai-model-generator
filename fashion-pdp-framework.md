# Fashion PDP Visual Framework
### Evidence-Based Standards for AI Fashion Photography Generation

Research compiled March 2026. Sources: Baymard Institute, Nielsen Norman Group, Looklet, Path Edits (top-25 brand analysis), ScienceDirect, Shopify CRO research, industry photography guides.

---

## 1. The Universal PDP Neutral

**The industry-standard default hero pose is not mannequin-neutral. It is a controlled contrapposto stance.**

### Precise Definition

- **Weight distribution:** 70% on the rear/dominant leg, 30% on the lead foot which is placed slightly forward and to the side (approximately 20–30cm gap between feet)
- **Hip:** Slight shift (10–15°) toward the weight-bearing side — creates the natural S-curve silhouette
- **Torso:** Subtly counter-rotated against the hip, shoulders level or with very slight drop on the weight-bearing side
- **Arms:** One arm falls naturally at the side with a slight bend at the elbow (hand relaxed, fingers softly curled — not a fist, not splayed). Opposite arm raised slightly with hand resting at the upper hip or just touching the waistband
- **Hands:** Never rigid. Fingers relaxed, slightly separated. No jazz hands. No closed fist. The most common default: one hand lightly grazing the hip seam
- **Feet:** Lead foot pointed forward or turned slightly outward (15°). Rear foot turned outward (30–45°). Small heel-to-arch stagger
- **Chin:** Level or tilted 5° downward — never chin-up (looks aggressive) or chin on chest (looks defeated)
- **Gaze:** Direct into lens for accessible/mid-market. Slightly off-lens (3–5° to the left of camera) for editorial/premium

**Prompt-ready description:**
> Standing in a relaxed contrapposto stance, weight shifted onto the right leg with hip slightly raised on that side, left foot stepped forward and turned slightly outward, left knee softly bent. Right arm falls naturally at the side, hand relaxed at the hip. Left hand lightly rests at the waist. Shoulders level, chin slightly down, direct gaze into camera. Neutral-to-slight-smile expression.

### What it Is Not
- NOT the T-pose (arms horizontal) — this is a rigging default, not a fashion pose
- NOT mannequin stance (legs together, arms rigid at sides) — sterile, zero engagement
- NOT extreme hip pop (too casual/editorial for neutral hero)
- NOT crossed arms (closed body language)

## 2. Pose Standards by Garment Category

| Category | Weight / Stance | Arm / Hand Position | Hip / Torso | Expression | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dresses / Skirts** | Weight on one leg, lead leg steps forward, slight knee bend to show movement in skirt hem | One hand lightly at hip seam, other at side or touching fabric | Slight hip turn (15–20°) toward camera to reveal silhouette from front | Soft neutral or very slight smile | Flowing, feminine, unhurried |
| **Tops / Blouses** | Standard contrapposto, feet shoulder-width apart | Both arms slightly away from torso (5–8cm gap) to reveal sleeve line; hands relaxed or one grazing the waistband | Minimal hip engagement — keep torso facing forward to maximize garment real estate | Neutral, direct gaze | Clean, informational |
| **Trousers / Jeans** | Weight on rear leg, lead leg steps forward 30–40cm, lead knee slightly bent to show trouser break at shoe | Arms dropped slightly wider than standard, thumbs may hook into front pockets | Hip shift to weight-bearing side shows trouser drape and leg line | Neutral or slight smile | Confident, grounded |
| **Outerwear / Jackets** | Slight 3/4 turn (20–30°) from the waist to show layering depth; feet in standard stagger | Jacket open or held open with one hand at lapel; other arm at side | Shoulders slightly broader stance to convey structure | Direct, neutral | Elevated, structured |
| **Knitwear / Loungewear** | Weight forward, relaxed lean — may sit on stool or lean against surface | Arms soft and close to body, one may be crossed loosely at waist; hands gentle | Minimal hip engagement; posture slightly relaxed (not hunched) | Warm smile or relaxed close-mouth smile | Cozy, approachable |
| **Activewear** | Power stance: feet hip-width or wider, slight bend at knees (athletic ready position) OR dynamic walking/mid-stride pose | Arms in motion-implied position — one arm forward, one back; or hands at hips in a strong V | Core engaged, upright posture, weight evenly distributed or in stride | Strong, focused expression (mouth closed, direct gaze) or slight exertion expression | Energetic, strong, performance-implied |
| **Occasion / Formal** | Feet together or very narrow stagger, upright posture — no exaggerated hip shift | Arms close to body, hands relaxed at sides or one hand holding clutch/prop | Torso square-on to camera, elongated neck, elevated chin (5° up vs. standard neutral) | Composed neutral or closed-mouth smile | Poised, elegant, aspirational |
| **Swimwear / Lingerie** | Open, confident stance — feet shoulder-width, slight hip shift, weight forward | Arms naturally at sides or one hand at hip; avoid covering body | Hip angle toward camera; open posture; never arms crossed or self-covering gestures | Warm, confident, direct gaze | Assured, body-positive, natural |

## 3. Styling Energy Profiles

Six named profiles mapped to brand tier and aesthetic. Each is usable as a prompt modifier.

---

### Profile 1: MINIMAL CLARITY
**Brands:** COS, Uniqlo, Arket, Muji
**Core body language:** Stillness with purpose. No extraneous gesture. The garment is the communication.

- **Stance:** Standard contrapposto but dialed to its subtlest — minimal hip shift, feet barely staggered
- **Arms:** Both arms at sides, hands fully relaxed and just off the thigh
- **Hands:** Fingers loosely extended, no touching face or hair
- **Gaze:** Directly into lens, no expression modulation — blank but not cold
- **Expression:** Mouth closed, neutral. Zero smile. Not stern — simply still.
- **Energy level:** 2/10. The model does not compete with the garment.

**Prompt modifier:** *"Standing in a minimal contrapposto, hands relaxed at sides, direct gaze into lens, neutral closed-mouth expression, still and composed, no gesture, Scandinavian-minimal editorial energy"*

---

### Profile 2: ACCESSIBLE WARMTH
**Brands:** H&M, ASOS, Next, Marks & Spencer
**Core body language:** Approachable, friendly, non-intimidating. I look like someone you know.

- **Stance:** Standard contrapposto with slightly wider foot stance for groundedness
- **Arms:** One arm at side, one hand resting at hip or loosely holding opposite elbow
- **Hands:** Relaxed, slightly active — may touch waistband lightly
- **Gaze:** Direct into lens
- **Expression:** Genuine slight smile — lips slightly parted, eyes engaged (Duchenne smile cues). Not a grin. Not a beam. A smile.
- **Energy level:** 5/10. Engaged but not performative.

**Prompt modifier:** *"Relaxed contrapposto stance, one hand lightly at hip, direct gaze into camera, genuine soft smile with slightly parted lips, warm and approachable energy, natural and unpretentious"*

---

### Profile 3: EDITORIAL COOL
**Brands:** Zara, Mango, & Other Stories, Reformation, Weekday
**Core body language:** Effortless cool. Movement-implied. The model is caught mid-thought, not posed.

- **Stance:** Less symmetric — more pronounced hip shift, torso slightly angled away from camera while face turns back toward lens (over-the-shoulder implication)
- **Arms:** More gestural — one arm may be reaching, adjusting collar, or mid-movement
- **Hands:** Active — touching hair, adjusting waistband, or in mid-swing
- **Gaze:** Slightly off-lens (3–5° away), or mid-glance, as if caught in a moment
- **Expression:** Variable — may be neutral-serious, mid-laugh, or looking away entirely. Intentional inconsistency.
- **Energy level:** 7/10. Fashion-forward, self-aware, a little unpredictable.

**Prompt modifier:** *"Slightly asymmetric stance with pronounced hip shift, torso angled 15 degrees away from camera, face turning back toward lens, one hand loosely adjusting the neckline, gaze 5 degrees off-lens to the left, editorial fashion energy, effortlessly cool and slightly unposed"*

---

### Profile 4: PREMIUM POISE
**Brands:** Sandro, Maje, Reiss, Ted Baker, Whistles, Phase Eight
**Core body language:** Quiet confidence. Nothing to prove. Elevated bearing.

- **Stance:** Upright, elongated — model appears taller due to posture. Slight contrapposto but controlled
- **Arms:** One arm dropped at side with a deliberate hand position (not touching clothing). One arm may be lightly bent with hand at lower abdomen
- **Hands:** The most deliberate of all profiles — fingers slightly extended and separated, as if the hand itself is styled
- **Gaze:** Slightly off-lens, or direct but with heavy-lidded quality (not sleepy — calculated)
- **Expression:** Neutral, mouth closed. For luxury: neutral expression increases perceived social distance, which enhances luxury valuation (ScienceDirect, 2021)
- **Energy level:** 3/10. Controlled stillness signals desirability.

**Prompt modifier:** *"Upright elongated stance with controlled contrapposto, one arm at side with deliberately relaxed hand, gaze slightly off-lens to the left, neutral closed-mouth expression with composed bearing, premium fashion editorial energy, quiet confidence"*

---

### Profile 5: STREET ENERGY
**Brands:** Urban Outfitters, ASOS Design, Monki, Carhartt WIP, Palace
**Core body language:** Loose, casual, self-expressive. The clothes belong to a person, not a hanger.

- **Stance:** Variable — may be mid-walk, leaning against wall, or sitting on a surface. Asymmetric and relaxed
- **Arms:** Hands in pockets, or arms loosely swinging. Hoodie strings being played with. Casual, tactile
- **Hands:** In pockets (thumbs out or full pocket), or casually at sides
- **Gaze:** Often looking away or down — non-direct gaze is common and intentional
- **Expression:** Natural laugh, dead-pan cool, or distracted look. Smile only if it's a real laugh.
- **Energy level:** 6/10. Casual but deliberate.

**Prompt modifier:** *"Relaxed asymmetric stance, hands loosely in pockets or at sides, gaze directed slightly away from camera, natural unstudied expression, casual street energy, urban fashion editorial"*

---

### Profile 6: ATHLETIC PERFORMANCE
**Brands:** Lululemon, Gymshark, Alo Yoga, Sweaty Betty, Adidas
**Core body language:** Physical capability on display. Strong, functional, embodied.

- **Stance:** Athletic ready position — feet hip-width or slightly wider, knees soft, core engaged, OR mid-stride dynamic pose
- **Arms:** Position implies movement — one forward, one back in stride; or hands braced on knees; or arms extended in stretch position
- **Hands:** Relaxed but purposeful — no jazz hands, no fists. May grip clothing or equipment
- **Gaze:** Forward and focused. Direct. Often slightly upward (implies aspiration/effort)
- **Expression:** Determined neutral, or slight exertion expression (not grimace). No forced smiles.
- **Energy level:** 8–9/10. Movement and capability are the message.

**Prompt modifier:** *"Athletic stance with feet hip-width apart, slight knee bend, core engaged, arms in a dynamic stride or power position, forward-focused determined gaze, strong performance energy, activewear photography"*

## 4. Angle and Image Set Standards

### Three-Quarter Turn: The Actual Degree

The industry-standard "three-quarter" angle is **45 degrees** — confirmed across multiple ecommerce photography sources. This is also called the 3/4 or diagonal angle. It is NOT 30 degrees.

At 45°: two faces of the garment are visible simultaneously, drape and dimensional form are revealed, and the body reads as naturally three-dimensional.

### Standard Angle Set (Our Current Architecture — Validated)

| Angle | Body Turn | Camera Height | Primary Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Front** | 0° — facing camera | Eye level | Hero shot, first impression, overall silhouette |
| **Three-Quarter** | 45° body turn left or right | Eye level | Drape, dimensionality, garment texture and movement |
| **Back** | 180° — facing away | Eye level | Back design, hem length confirmation, rear fit |

This three-angle set is the validated industry standard. Front + 3/4 + back covers the complete garment with no redundancy.

### When is Back View Mandatory?

| Category | Back View Priority |
|---|---|
| Outerwear / Jackets | **High** — back construction, hem, vent detail |
| Dresses / Occasion wear | **High** — back neckline, zip, open-back designs |
| Swimwear | **High** — back coverage is a primary purchase factor |
| Knitwear | **Medium** — back hem length, back seam, tag-free details |
| Trousers / Jeans | **Medium** — rear fit, pocket placement, rise |
| Tops / Blouses | **Medium-Low** — back view adds value only if back design exists |
| Activewear | **High** — back coverage, waistband, seam construction |

### Extended Image Set (Beyond Hero Three-Angle)

Top fashion brands average **8.36 images per product** (Path Edits analysis of top 25 fashion brands). Recommended full set:

| # | Image Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Front hero | Standard contrapposto, full-length, brand profile energy |
| 2 | Three-quarter | 45° turn, same model and outfit |
| 3 | Back | Full-length rear view |
| 4 | Detail close-up | Fabric texture, stitching, hardware, print detail |
| 5 | Flat lay | Overhead or angled flat, shows garment construction |
| 6 | Scale/fit shot | Mid-length crop showing fit at waist, hip, or hem |
| 7 | Lifestyle context | Model in a setting relevant to garment use-case |
| 8+ | Colour variants | Same angle, different colorways |

**Minimum viable set for Shopify PDP:** Images 1, 2, 3, 4 (hero + 3/4 + back + detail). Below 3 images causes trust failure (Baymard Institute).

### Image Ordering Convention

Hero (front on-model) → three-quarter → back → detail → flat lay → lifestyle. Always lead with the on-model front shot. Never lead with a flat lay.

## 5. Background and Lighting Standards by Brand Tier

### Industry Baseline Data
- **94.26%** of fashion product images use plain/solid backgrounds (Path Edits, top 25 brand analysis)
- **88.98%** of those plain backgrounds are white (various shades)
- Only **1.53%** of product images are contextual/lifestyle

This means the industry's strong default is: **white or near-white studio background for product images, lifestyle reserved for secondary gallery frames and marketing assets.**

### Background Standards by Tier

| Tier | Background | Lighting | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mass market** (ASOS, H&M, Primark) | Pure white (#FFFFFF or 250–255 RGB) | Soft, flat, even — shadowless or with subtle floor shadow | Marketplace compliant (Amazon mandates white), fastest visual scan, maximum clarity |
| **Mid-market** (Reformation, & Other Stories, Reiss) | Off-white or warm white (240–250 RGB), sometimes very light grey | Soft directional light from one side — creates subtle shadow for depth | Warmer, slightly more premium feel without losing clarity. Slight texture to background acceptable |
| **Premium / contemporary luxury** (Sandro, Maje, Ted Baker) | Soft grey (#E8E8E8–#D0D0D0), or warm cream/stone | Directional studio light with gentle shadow — more sculptural | Differentiates from mass market; creates mood without lifestyle context |
| **Editorial / streetwear** (Zara, Urban Outfitters, Weekday) | Variable — may be white, grey, concrete texture, or outdoor environment | Varied — flat studio OR high-contrast directional OR natural outdoor | Brand signals creativity and energy; inconsistency across products is intentional |
| **Activewear** (Lululemon, Gymshark, Alo) | White or very light grey for hero; lifestyle/gym environment for secondary | High-contrast directional light — sculpts the body, highlights fabric texture and seams | Performance apparel needs to show construction; lifestyle setting communicates use-case |

### Lighting Standards (Prompt-Level)

**Standard studio (mass/mid-market):**
> Soft even studio lighting, subtle shadow on the floor, no harsh shadows on garment, neutral white background

**Premium directional:**
> Soft directional studio lighting from camera-left at 45 degrees, gentle shadow falling right, slight dimensionality, off-white background

**Editorial high-contrast:**
> High-contrast directional lighting, strong single source from above-left, defined shadows, stark white or light grey background

## 6. Key Conversion Insights

Evidence-based findings that directly inform product decisions:

### 1. On-Model vs. Flat Lay: Conditional, Not Absolute
There is no universal winner. **Execution quality matters more than format choice.** However: on-model imagery dramatically outperforms flat lay for fit-driven categories (dresses, trousers, swimwear). Ghost mannequin outperforms flat lay for structured garments (blazers, outerwear). Flat lay performs adequately for simple casual items at low price points.

**Practical rule:** Any garment where "does this fit my body" is a purchase question requires on-model photography.

### 2. On-Model AI Imagery Works: 157% Conversion Increase
Milaner (luxury retailer) tested AI-powered on-model imagery vs. flat lay and achieved a **157% increase in conversion rate** and **40% boost in shopper engagement**. This validates AI-generated on-model photography as commercially effective, not a compromise.

### 3. Multiple Angles Materially Lift Sales
- DueMaternity: **+27% sales** from rotating multi-angle image gallery
- Grainger: **+47% conversion** from 360° spin feature
- **Insight:** Each additional angle reduces a dimension of purchase doubt. Front removes silhouette doubt. 3/4 removes depth/drape doubt. Back removes rear-design and hem doubt. Detail removes quality doubt.

### 4. Model Expression: Smile for Mid-Market, Neutral for Luxury
A fashion retailer A/B test found: **slight smile → +18% click-through rate, +21% sales** vs. neutral expression (PiktID, 2024).

However, ScienceDirect (2021) research confirms: **neutral expression increases perceived social distance, which enhances luxury product valuation.** Smiling models reduce perceived premium for high-end products.

**Decision rule:** Use slight smile for accessible/mid-market (H&M, ASOS energy). Use neutral expression for premium/contemporary luxury (Sandro, Reiss energy).

### 5. 83% of Consumers Say Images Are Extremely Influential
83% of U.S. consumers report that high-quality product imagery is "extremely influential" in purchase decisions (Convertcart, 2025). Image quality is the #1 conversion lever for fashion — more than price display, reviews, or descriptions.

### 6. Minimum 3 Images to Avoid Trust Failure; 5–8 Is the Standard
Baymard Institute usability research: fewer than 3 images "makes users uneasy" and triggers site abandonment. Top 25 brands average **8.36 images per product**. Shoppers "trust five images, love eight."

**For Shopify brands:** Minimum viable = 3 (front, back, detail). Target = 5–8.

### 7. 94% of Fashion Images Use Plain Backgrounds — White Dominates
Only 1.53% of product images in the top 25 brand analysis were lifestyle/contextual. White or near-white backgrounds dominate because they: (a) are marketplace-compliant, (b) enable faster visual comparison across thumbnails, (c) reduce cognitive load. **Lifestyle images are secondary gallery assets, not hero frames.**

eBay and BigCommerce data: listings with white backgrounds receive **up to 20% more clicks** than cluttered or colored backgrounds.

### 8. Lifestyle Images Can Lift Conversion by Up to 40% — As Secondary Images
Lifestyle imagery can increase conversions by up to 40% compared to plain product shots — but this is when paired with a clean hero, not replacing it. **The hierarchy is:** white studio hero first, lifestyle context second.

### 9. Model Diversity: +71% Trust Increase, Directly Impacts Purchases
71% trust increase when brands feature plus-size or diverse models (industry research). Nielsen Norman Group usability testing found shoppers actively appreciated and noticed diverse model casting, with direct statements like "they're showing someone that looks like me, which is important."

**Practical implication:** Model diversity is a trust signal, not a nice-to-have. For brands targeting broad demographics, diverse casting directly reduces purchase hesitation.

### 10. The 45-Degree Three-Quarter Is the Universal Standard
The "three-quarter" angle across the industry is **45 degrees**, not 30°. It mimics natural human perception of objects and creates dimensional form. This is confirmed as the standard across ASOS, H&M, and major fashion retailers globally.

## 7. The Complete PDP Image Set — What "Done" Looks Like

### Minimum Viable (3 images)
1. Front hero — on-model, full-length, standard contrapposto
2. Back — on-model, full-length
3. Detail close-up — fabric texture or key design feature

### Standard (5–8 images) — Recommended for Shopify
1. Front hero — on-model, full-length
2. Three-quarter (45°) — on-model
3. Back — on-model, full-length
4. Detail close-up — fabric / construction / print
5. Flat lay — overhead or angled
6. Scale/fit crop — mid-body showing fit at key measurement point
7. Lifestyle — model in context (optional but high-value)
8. Colorway alternate — same hero pose in second colorway (if applicable)

### Image Ordering Convention (Never Deviate)
**Hero always first.** On-model front shot is image #1 in every gallery. Category thumbnail = image #1. This is the universal Shopify and marketplace convention.

Order: Front (hero) → 3/4 → Back → Detail → Flat lay → Lifestyle → Variants

### What Top-Converting Shopify Fashion Stores Have in Common
- Large, high-quality images that dominate the PDP layout
- Multiple angles including at least front + back
- On-model shots as primary images (not flat lay)
- Zoom functionality (100% of top brands)
- Consistent background and lighting across all SKUs in the same collection
- Video or motion content where budget allows (28% of top brands use video)
